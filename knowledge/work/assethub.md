# The company and my role

AssetHub is an AI-powered 3D platform, a Techstars company: an AI-native 3D editor that takes you from concept art to production-ready 3D models, with AI mesh generation and texture refinement. Around when I joined, it had raised a seed round of about two million dollars. I was a full-stack engineer there from September 2025 to September 2026.

# What I built

I built customer-facing workflows and internal tooling for generating and processing 3D assets with AI, and worked across the React and TypeScript interfaces and the backend services behind the core 3D editor. The editor takes a team from concept art to a production-ready model in one place: generate a mesh with AI, refine its textures and keep iterating without leaving the browser. We shipped fast and iterated with customers, and in 8 months the platform grew to more than 11,000 users and $5K in monthly recurring revenue.

Two things I built that I'm proud of:

- A 3D composer agent. Different AI providers are good at different things, so a character might come back as separate meshes from different models. The composer agent takes meshes generated by different AI providers and combines them into one single character mesh, so you get one usable asset instead of a pile of parts.
- GPU-driven face selection for the editor, explained below.

# Hard problems and decisions

Most 3D editors break down when meshes get really dense: try selecting faces on a mesh with millions of triangles and it quickly becomes slow or unusable, because picking is done with raycasting on the CPU. So I built face selection that runs on the GPU instead, in the browser, with hover, box selection and lasso selection; it can support any bounded selection shape while staying interactive on very large meshes.

How it works: when selection mode turns on, the mesh is converted to non-indexed geometry in the background, so every triangle has its own face ID. A WebGPU render pass then draws the mesh into an offscreen framebuffer, writing identifiers instead of colors: the geometry index goes in the red channel, which handles scenes with several meshes, and the face index in the green channel. That gives a texture that maps every pixel to the triangle under it. When the mouse moves, the pointer position is normalized and used to sample that buffer, which instantly gives the face under the cursor, and box or lasso selection reads the faces inside the region. From there the face index flows through the shader pipeline for highlighting, previews and selection feedback. The result is smooth interaction even on meshes with millions of faces. I built it with WebGPU and TSL, Three.js's shading language, and wrote it up as a post on LinkedIn.
